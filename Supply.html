The Law of Supply

The law of supply is a vital economic principle saying that as the price of a good or service increases, the quantity supplied by producers increases,

On the other end, as the price decreases, the quantity supplied decreases.

This law is utilized to maximize profits in a positive correlation with price.

Supply Graph

Image by FreePik

This Graph shows that as Price increases, Quantity Supplied also increases.

This is because producers are willing to supply more of a good or service when they can sell it at a higher price.

On the contrary, if the price decreases, the quantity supplied also decreases.

This is because producers are less willing to supply a good or service when they can sell it at a lower price.

Shifters

Image by FreePik

Shifters are factors that can cause the supply curve to shift.

Shifters for supply include changes in input prices, technology, and the number of sellers.

it also includes laborer wage and subsidies.

For example, if the price of an input increases, the supply curve will shift to the left, indicating a decrease in supply.

On the other hand, if the price of an input decreases, the supply curve will shift to the right, indicating an increase in supply.
